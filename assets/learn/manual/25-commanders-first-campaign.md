# First twenty turns: the commander's campaign

VOIDREIGN is won by decisions that reinforce one another. This opening plan works as a teaching framework, not a fixed recipe: enemy contact, map geography, colonisable planets and new discoveries can change the best move.

To play with live guidance, choose **Tutorial** from the title screen, then **Commander's First Campaign**. Finish the seven introductory lessons first if you are unfamiliar with the windows. You can also practise without restrictions in the **Frontier Command** training scenario.

## The first turn

1. Open [Empire Status](window:empire-status) (`F11`) and read the mineral, organic and radioactive balances. A positive treasury is not the same as sustainable net production.
2. Open [Construction Queues](window:queues) (`F7`) and start a **colony ship** at the homeworld. Avoid filling the queue with expensive military designs before assessing upkeep.
3. Open [Research](window:research) (`F8`); queue two technologies with a concrete purpose. Focus the first until complete if speed matters.
4. Assign the **Explore** order to a mobile scout. Check for alternative systems and warp connections, not only habitable planets.
5. Open [Planets](window:planets) (`F4`), choose a colonisable planet and order the starting colony ship there.
6. Check that your build and orders are actually present, then end the turn with `F12`.

Read the [main window](main-window), [economy](economy), [research](research) and [construction](construction) chapters for the controls.

## Turns two to five

- Read the Log at each turn. If the scout arrived, send it exploring again. A ship left without orders does not scout on its own.
- When your colony ship arrives, found the colony; check the colony's atmosphere, mineral values and connection to the rest of your empire.
- Remember that the spaceport network controls when system production becomes available empire-wide. Remote colonies without a spaceport can be strategically valuable but should not be treated as equivalent to a developed home system.
- Review every construction queue. Choose economic or research infrastructure when new upkeep threatens to outrun production.
- Do not automatically expand into the first red-star domed world if a comparable breathable world is accessible.

See [colonisation](planets-and-colonies), [the turn cycle](turns) and [logistics](logistics).

## Turns six to ten

Your first wave of expansion should be paying for the second. Balance additional colony ships against useful facilities, research and fleet supply.

- Look at net production separately for minerals, organics and radioactives. Pause optional spending if any resource is falling toward zero.
- Move construction to well-developed yards and keep research aligned with what the empire can actually build.
- Form a fleet if concentration protects a warp choke point; keep independent scouts separate when speed and discovery matter more.
- When another empire is encountered, inspect its mood and treaty. A non-aggression proposal can buy time; it is not an assurance of permanent peace.

See [ships and fleets](ships-and-fleets), [diplomacy](diplomacy) and [intelligence](intelligence).

## Turns eleven to twenty

Priorities depend on geography and rivals, but these checks keep you resilient:

1. Identify one important border or warp point. Position defenses where they protect colonies and supply routes, not on every empty sector.
2. Test a new combat design with the Combat Simulator before paying to build a fleet of it.
3. Keep colonial queues productive and research moving; use the budget rather than the size of your fleet as your only indicator of strength.
4. Explore new systems deliberately. Chart alternate routes to avoid being trapped behind a hostile empire.
5. Reassess treaties and enemy power before beginning a war. An invasion also requires troop transport and control of the target system.

Read [combat](combat), [combat windows](combat-windows) and [ground combat](ground-combat-and-capture).

## Economic triage

| Symptom | First question | Common response |
|---|---|---|
| Minerals falling | Are shipbuilding and maintenance consuming more than production? | Delay expensive ships; add mineral production |
| Organics falling | Is population and fleet demand overtaking food production? | Strengthen organics facilities and lower avoidable costs |
| Radioactives falling | Are energy-intensive facilities and ships overextended? | Build radioactive extraction or delay maintenance-heavy designs |
| Construction stalled | Can the treasury pay all three costs? | Reduce competing commitments and allow storage to recover |
| Remote colonies weak | Do they have population, facilities and a useful spaceport network? | Develop the economic foundation before treating them as major shipyards |
| Research slow | Are points spread thinly, or are research facilities absent? | Focus a practical project; add research capacity |

Balance is situational. Building storage capacity and production are different decisions: storage prevents losing surpluses at a cap, but extra storage cannot correct a persistent deficit. Check the rules and facilities available in the current version rather than assuming every empire has identical access.

## Ship design and deterrence

Two specialised ships can outperform two generalists when the fleet has a real job: scouts should travel efficiently, escorts should protect vulnerable expansion, and siege ships should have a target worth their cost. Supply and repairs matter between engagements. Formation, weapons range, sensors, shields and armor are all design considerations.

## Contact and first war

First contact is information, not a command to fight. Compare borders, trade value, relative technology and how quickly reinforcements can arrive. Defend one strategically useful entry system before building a dispersed navy. During a battle, review movement, weapons and damage; do not rely on auto-combat to fix a badly designed fleet.

## A decision checklist

Before every `F12`, ask:

1. Did I read the Log and respond to emergencies?
2. Are exploration and colonisation ships doing useful work?
3. Are my construction and research queues occupied with achievable jobs?
4. Can I pay maintenance next turn without exhausting critical resources?
5. Is my weakest border adequately watched or defended?
6. Has anything changed that makes last turn's plan wrong?

> Your objective is not to maximise a single number. It is to build an empire able to make meaningful choices when the galaxy surprises you.
